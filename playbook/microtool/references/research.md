# Phase 0: validate the idea

Goal: decide in one sitting whether a tool idea is worth building, and write the evidence into README.md's
"Why this idea" section so it's never re-researched. Use web search; say what is verified and what isn't.

## 1. Demand: do people search for this?

- Google autocomplete for the seed phrase and variations: "<x> generator", "<x> calculator", "<x> online free",
  "how to <x>", "<x> for <platform/language/country>". Note the exact phrasings.
- People Also Ask and related searches on the results page.
- YouTube autocomplete if the audience is creators.
- Search volume: Google Keyword Planner (free with an Ads account) or a free tier tool. If not checked, write
  "volumes not verified" rather than guessing numbers. Autocomplete proves people search, not how many.

## 2. Supply: what ranks now?

For the 5–10 best queries, look at page 1 and classify each result:
- **Articles only** for a "generator/calculator" query → gap (people want a tool, get a listicle).
- **Weak tools** (ad-stuffed, broken on mobile, signup walls, outdated) → beatable.
- **Strong tools from big brands** (Canva, Omni, Adobe) → avoid head terms; look for a sub-niche.
- **Spam / mirror pages** for regional or niche variants → biggest gap.

The wedge is usually one of: a tool where only articles exist, a niche the big tools ignore (language, platform,
profession), or doing the job without signup/upload.

## 3. Money

- Audience countries → RPM range. Rough: US/UK/CA/AU display RPM ~$5–20 per 1,000 pageviews (higher in finance,
  insurance, energy, B2B software; lower in entertainment); South Asia often under $1–2. Treat as ballpark.
- Buying intent nearby? (products people buy after using the tool → affiliate potential)
- Seasonality (storms, tax season, holidays, exams) → when to publish.

## 4. Build cost and moat

- Can it run fully in the browser? (no backend is the rule)
- Data needed, and can it be checked against primary sources? Licences (fonts, images, datasets) allow redistribution?
- What makes it better than the top result in one sentence?
- Content plan: at least ~20 genuinely distinct pages possible (tool variants, niche landing pages, guides)?
  Below that, AdSense approval and long-term traffic are hard.

## 5. Write it down

In README.md "Why this idea (research summary, <date>)": Demand (queries found), Supply (what ranks, the gap),
Money (audience, RPM, affiliate), Verdict (go/no-go + the wedge in one sentence), Not verified yet.
Then draft the V1 feature list and the "What we will NOT build" list (the crowded parts to stay out of).

Give a straight recommendation. A no-go with reasons is a good outcome; it saves weeks.
